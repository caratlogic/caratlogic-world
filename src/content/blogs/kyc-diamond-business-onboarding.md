## Why customer and supplier onboarding both matter
A diamond business may receive an offer from a new supplier in the morning and prepare a quotation for a new customer that afternoon. Both relationships can involve valuable goods, business documents, payment arrangements, and follow-up. If details are scattered across emails, spreadsheets, and folders, it can be difficult to tell what has been received, what still needs review, or which documents need updating.

That is where a clear **customer and supplier onboarding process** helps. It gives the business a consistent way to collect and review information before a relationship becomes active, then keep relevant records organized over time. KYC means “Know Your Customer,” but in the jewelry trade it can also involve knowing the suppliers and other business partners you work with.

The process should fit the relationship. A new customer and a new supplier may need to provide different information, and the checks required can depend on the business, the transaction, and applicable rules. A single form for everyone may ask too much of some contacts and too little of others.

## What KYC means for a diamond business
For a diamond or gem business, KYC is a process for learning who a business partner is and keeping a suitable record of that relationship. It can include collecting company details, reviewing documents, recording the purpose of the business relationship, and following up when information is missing or out of date.

KYC is not the same as simply saving a copy of a license. The business needs a defined way to decide what information to request, who reviews it, how to record the review, and when to revisit the file. The right process should match the business’s own policies and the rules that apply to it.

The Responsible Jewellery Council’s 2024 Code of Practices includes a Know Your Counterparty provision for relevant suppliers and customers of materials within the RJC’s scope. It also calls for enhanced KYC for members handling covered materials on consignment. This is an industry standard with a defined scope; it should not be presented as a rule that applies identically to every US jewelry business.[responsiblejewellery.com](https://www.responsiblejewellery.com/wp-content/uploads/RJC-2024-COP-Standard.pdf)

In the United States, FinCEN guidance discusses risk assessment involving foreign suppliers for certain covered dealers in precious metals, precious stones, or jewels. Whether a particular business is covered depends on its circumstances and the relevant rules, so businesses should confirm their obligations with a qualified compliance or legal professional.[FinCEN.gov](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/guidance-dealers-including-certain-retailers-precious)

## Customer onboarding and supplier onboarding are different
A customer onboarding process helps the business understand who it is selling to and how the relationship will work. Depending on its own policy and the transaction, the business might ask for the customer’s legal business name, business address, registration details, contact person, and information about the person authorized to act for the company. Payment arrangements or other details may also be relevant to the relationship.

Supplier onboarding has a different focus. A diamond dealer may need to understand what the supplier provides, where the business operates, which licenses or certificates are relevant, and what sourcing or supply chain information the business requires. A manufacturer may need a process that reflects its own suppliers, materials, and production arrangements.

That does not mean every supplier or customer should receive a long questionnaire. A better starting point is to decide what information is relevant to each relationship, then request only what the business needs under its process. Forms should be easy to understand, and document requests should explain what the business is asking for.

## A practical supplier onboarding process
A useful supplier onboarding process can be organized into five steps:

1.  **Decide what information you need.** Set the questions and documents your business requests from a new supplier. Consider the supplier’s role, the type of goods involved, and your own policies.
2.  **Send a clear request.** Explain why the information is requested, how to complete the form, and where to upload supporting documents.
3.  **Review the submission.** Check whether required fields and documents are present. Record who reviewed the information and whether anything needs follow-up.
4.  **Request missing details.** Tell the supplier exactly what is missing and how to send it. Keep the request and response with the submission.
5.  **Keep the record current.** Track documents that expire or need renewal, and follow your policy for periodic review.

The same outline can help with customer onboarding, while the questions and documents are adjusted for customers. A consistent process helps staff follow the same steps without treating every relationship as identical.

## Keep the records useful after onboarding
A completed form is only one part of the record. Staff should be able to identify the customer or supplier, find the submitted documents, see whether a review is complete, and locate any follow-up requests. When a document expires or a business detail changes, the record should make it clear that an update is needed.

A basic internal process can assign responsibility for each step: who sends the invitation, who checks the submission, who requests missing information, and who follows up on renewals. This reduces uncertainty when different team members handle the same relationship.

Businesses should also decide how long to keep records, who can access them, and how sensitive documents are protected. Applicable requirements can vary, so a software workflow should support the business’s approved policy rather than replace it.

## Questions diamond business owners often ask
### Should I onboard both customers and suppliers?
Your business can create processes for both. The information you request may differ by relationship, transaction, and the policies or rules that apply to your business.

### Does completing a KYC form prove that a business is compliant?
No. A form helps collect and organize information. It does not, on its own, confirm that every detail is accurate or satisfy every legal, regulatory, or industry requirement.

### How often should I update customer and supplier records?
There is no single schedule that fits every business. Set review and renewal procedures that match your policies, the documents involved, and the requirements that apply to your business.

### Can software decide whether a customer or supplier is approved?
Software can organize submissions and support a review workflow. Your business should define who reviews the information and makes the decision.
