## How to Choose KYC and Onboarding Software for a Diamond Business
Diamond businesses often collect customer and supplier information through a mix of forms, email attachments, spreadsheets, and follow-up messages. That can make it difficult to see which information has been submitted, which documents still need review, and who is responsible for the next step.

KYC and onboarding software can organize parts of this process. A suitable system may let a business create forms, invite customers or suppliers to complete them, collect supporting documents, review submissions, and track follow-up. The right choice depends on how your business works and what you need the software to handle.

Start by looking at your actual onboarding process. A diamond wholesaler may need a different workflow from a manufacturer, retailer, or business that regularly handles consignment goods. Before comparing software, write down what information you collect, who reviews it, how you request missing details, and how you keep records current.

## First, define what “KYC” means for your business
“KYC” is often used as shorthand for collecting and reviewing information about customers or business partners. It does not describe one universal checklist that every diamond business must follow. Requirements can depend on the business, its activities, its customers, and the markets where it operates.

In the United States, certain dealers in precious stones, jewels, and related goods may have AML obligations depending on their activities and whether they fall within the applicable rules. FinCEN’s guidance discusses the scope of these requirements and risk assessment for covered dealers. A software purchase does not determine whether your business is covered or make it compliant by itself. Confirm your obligations with a qualified legal or compliance professional.

For software evaluation, separate three things: information collection, document management, and the decisions your business makes after review. Software may support the first two and help record the third, but your team still needs to set the process and decide what is appropriate for each relationship.

## 1. Check that forms can match your workflow
A useful system should let you collect the information your business needs from both customers and suppliers. Check whether you can create separate forms or sections for different types of relationships, such as a new wholesale customer, a supplier, or another business contact.

Look for flexible field types, such as text, dropdowns, toggles, date fields, and document uploads. Also check whether you can mark specific questions as required, organize questions into sections, and explain why a document or detail is being requested.

The purpose is not to make the longest possible form. A form should ask for information that your business has a clear reason to collect. During a demo, try changing a form yourself. If every small edit requires vendor support, that may slow down routine changes to your process.

Ask how the system handles form versions. If you update a form after some customers have already received it, you should be able to understand which version each person received and what information they submitted.

## 2. Review the invitation and customer experience
The people completing a form may be busy and may not use your software every day. Check whether the invitation explains who is requesting the information, what the recipient needs to do, and where to ask questions.

Look at the form from the customer’s or supplier’s point of view. Is it easy to open on a phone or computer? Are the instructions clear? Can the person save progress and return later if the form is long? Does the system show which steps or fields remain incomplete?

Some systems use an email one-time passcode before a recipient can open an invited form. This can help limit access to someone who can access the invited email account. It should not be treated as proof of the person’s legal identity.

## 3. Look closely at document handling
Document upload is only one part of document management. Ask how the software organizes files, connects them to a customer or supplier record, and shows whether a document has been received, reviewed, or needs follow-up.

If your process tracks documents with expiry dates, check whether the system lets you record those dates and set reminders. Confirm whether you can see documents approaching expiry, identify the person responsible for follow-up, and mark a replacement document as received and reviewed.

Ask how the system handles earlier versions. Your team should be able to tell which file is current and whether an older file has been superseded. Also check whether uploaded documents can be downloaded or exported if your business changes systems or needs its own records.

A reminder is not a document review. Your team should still check any replacement according to its process and update the status accurately.

## 4. Make sure the review workflow is clear
After someone submits a form, your team needs to know what happens next. Check whether the software lets you assign submissions for review, record a status, request additional information, and see which submissions are waiting for action.

Statuses might include “In progress,” “Submitted,” “Under review,” “More information required,” “Approved,” or “Rejected.” The exact labels should fit your workflow. Ask whether you can configure them and whether the system records who changed a status and when.

Test a realistic review. Submit a sample form with one missing document. Then see whether the reviewer can request that item, whether the recipient can provide it, and whether the team can tell when the new file arrives. This helps reveal whether the software supports the full follow-up process or only the initial form submission.

## 5. Ask how software fits with your other systems
Many diamond businesses already use inventory, accounting, CRM, or order management software. Ask how the KYC system fits with the tools your team already uses.

Be specific about the connection you need. Do you simply want staff to access both systems with one login? Do you expect customer contact details to transfer between applications? Do you need documents or approval statuses to appear beside account records? These are different capabilities.

Ask the vendor to demonstrate the exact connection instead of relying on a general statement such as “integrated.” Confirm which data moves automatically, which steps remain manual, and whether the integration works in both directions. Do not assume that a KYC record automatically links to inventory, a memo, a quotation, or a sales restriction unless the vendor demonstrates that functionality and confirms it is supported.

## 6. Evaluate security, access, and data handling
Onboarding records may contain business and personal information. Ask how access is controlled, whether roles can limit who sees or edits documents, and how the vendor protects information while it is stored and transmitted.

Ask where records are hosted, how backups work, how your business can retrieve its information, and what happens to data if you stop using the software. Find out whether the vendor has a written process for security incidents and whether it uses other providers to store or process data.

Also ask whether you can set retention rules or remove records according to your policy. A system should support your information-handling process, but it does not decide how long your business is legally or operationally required to keep records. The Federal Trade Commission advises businesses to collect only the information they need, protect what they keep, and establish appropriate retention and disposal practices.

## 7. Compare usability, support, and total cost
A system may have many features but still be difficult for your staff or business partners to use. Ask for a practical demonstration with the employees who will build forms, review documents, and manage follow-up.

Compare the total cost, not just the starting subscription price. Check whether pricing depends on the number of users, forms, customers, storage, or features. Ask whether implementation, training, support, or data export carries an additional fee.

Find out what support is available when an invitation fails, a form needs changing, or a document cannot be uploaded. If you work with customers and suppliers in several countries, ask whether the forms can be presented in the languages your recipients need. Since your content and onboarding process may be English-first, check how translations can be added and maintained later.

## 8. Test the system using a real business scenario
Before choosing a platform, ask the vendor to demonstrate a complete workflow using a fictional diamond customer or supplier. For example:

1.  Create or select a form for a new business contact.
2.  Send an invitation to the right contact.
3.  Complete the form and upload a sample document.
4.  Save progress and return to finish the submission.
5.  Review the submission and request one missing item.
6.  Upload a replacement document and update its status.
7.  Set or view an expiry reminder.
8.  Find the current record and export the information.

If memo or consignment is part of your business, use that scenario to test what the software actually records and how it relates to the customer or supplier profile. Ask the vendor to demonstrate any connection you expect between onboarding and inventory or memo workflows.

## Choose software around the work your team needs to do
The best KYC and onboarding software for a diamond business is the system that supports the process your team actually follows. It should make forms easier to manage, help recipients submit information, show reviewers what needs attention, and keep current documents distinguishable from older ones.

Before buying, test the full process with a realistic customer and supplier example. Confirm the limits of integrations, ask how your information is protected and exported, and make sure the workflow does not claim to perform checks that your business still needs to complete. That gives you a clearer basis for choosing a system that fits your business.
